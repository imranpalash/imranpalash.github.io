      Race to Refuge

      Best Solo Project · OU Creaties Awards 2019

      A choose-your-path simulation of the U.S. refugee entry process. You play an applicant facing the system one conversation at a time, and each answer you give changes what happens next.

      1. 01You arrive as an applicant at a resettlement office.
      2. 02At each step, an officer speaks and you choose a response.
      3. 03A meter tracks how the conversation is going, and your choices set your path.

      Written, designed and produced by Imran Hasnat · Built in iSpring · Plays in the browser

        08 · AI

        AI, studied and used.

        I research how audiences judge AI-made media, teach students to use AI openly, and use it in my own production and data work, always with a human check before anything is published.

        Made with AI

        Real Enough

        Experimental film · ExpVideoFest, IAMCR 2026, Galway · Screened 1 July 2026

        An AI-generated film about the collapse of visual certainty. Photographs of protests, disasters and everyday life repeat and mutate across screens, and the audience is never told which images are real.

        When visual evidence becomes endlessly reproducible and subtly alterable, what exactly are we trusting when we look at a photograph?

        Role
        Written, directed and produced solo
        Made with
        Claude · Gemini · OpenAI · Meta AI · Cartesia
        Grew from
        My research on AI news credibility

        Studying it

        • AI news photos and credibility. An experiment on how image origin and colour shape the perceived credibility of AI-generated news photographs. Revista Interin, in press · IAMCR 2026, Galway · CJEN 2025, Dhaka
        • AI governance. How Asian technology awards confer legitimacy, and who they leave out. Asian Journal of Communication, in press
        All publications

        Teaching it

        • Students bring AI tools, AI agents and coding agents into production and data work where they fit the task.
        • Every assignment requires students to disclose how they used AI.
        • In class demonstrations, I show prototype video and synthetic voices made with AI, always labeled as AI.

        Using it My toolkit, by task

        Writing and editing

        Claude, for drafting and line editing long documents.

        ChatGPT and Gemini, for a second reading and counterarguments.

        Grammarly and DeepL Write, for a final check on grammar and tone.

        Transcription and translation

        Whisper, run through Hugging Face, for interview transcripts, checked against the audio before I quote anyone.

        Descript and Otter.ai, for editing podcasts and video from the transcript.

        DeepL and Google Translate, for first drafts in Bengali and Mandarin, which I revise by hand.

        Image, video and audio

        Nano Banana (Google Gemini), for generating the AI news photographs used in my credibility experiment.

        Adobe Firefly, with generative fill in Photoshop and Premiere Pro, for extending shots and cleanup.

        Adobe Podcast and DaVinci Resolve, for voice isolation and audio repair.

        Runway, Higgsfield AI and ElevenLabs, for prototype video and synthetic voices in class demonstrations, always labeled as AI.

        Cartesia, for the AI voice in Real Enough, my AI-generated film.

        Data and code

        Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot and Cline, for writing and debugging R and Python analysis scripts, and for web projects, including this site.

        Hugging Face and OpenRouter, for running open and custom models on specific tasks, and for comparing models side by side before choosing one.

        Research

        Elicit, Consensus and Semantic Scholar, for literature search. I read every source in full before citing it.

        NotebookLM and the AI assistants in NVivo and MAXQDA, for a first pass through papers and qualitative data, checked against a human codebook.

        The list changes often. I keep a tool when it saves time and its output holds up when checked.

        I am a researcher and educator who studies media, politics and all things digital, with a PhD in Mass Communication from the University of Oklahoma.
